Experiment 05 · Dragon Egg Chess

What if a dragon egg hatched on your chessboard?

  1. Hold more of the four center squares, even after your opponent replies.
  2. The egg warms toward you and hatches at 4. It starts one notch toward Black, who moves second.
  3. Your dragon moves like a queen and a knight. Pawns can't touch it.

Full rules

The twist

  1. An egg sits where d4, e4, d5 and e5 meet.
  2. After every move, if the player about to move holds the majority of those 4 squares, the egg warms one step toward them on a bar from −4 to +4. A majority only counts once it has survived an enemy move.
  3. White moves first, so the egg starts at −1, one step toward Black: White needs 5 warm-ups, Black 3.
  4. At +4 (White) or −4 (Black) the egg hatches: a Dragon appears on the nearest free center square. If all four are taken, it crushes the most valuable enemy piece on them; if that's the king, the game is over. If all four are yours, it hatches as soon as one frees up.
  5. The Dragon moves like a queen or a knight, and pawns can't capture it. One egg per game.

Base rules (every experiment)

  • Pieces move like normal chess.
  • No check, no checkmate: capture the enemy king to win.
  • Castling only needs an unmoved king and rook and an empty path.
  • 50 rounds without a capture, pawn move or destruction? The bigger army wins (equal armies: draw). Same after 150 rounds.
