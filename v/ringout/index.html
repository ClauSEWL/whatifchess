Experiment 10 · Ring-Out Chess

What if you could shove chess pieces off the board?

  1. Move into an enemy and you shove it one square back. No captures.
  2. Lines slide like dominoes. Whatever leaves the board is out.
  3. First to push out six wins. No ring-out for 20 rounds? The ring shrinks.

Full rules

The twist

  1. Pieces move normally. A move that would capture becomes a push: your piece takes the square and the enemy piece is shoved one square further along your move's direction (a knight pushes along its long leg). Pawns push only diagonally.
  2. Chain: if more pieces stand behind the pushed one (any color), the whole line slides one square. The piece at the end that would leave the board is out of the ring.
  3. Kings are too heavy to push: a push whose line contains a king is illegal. Kings are walls.
  4. Win by pushing out 6 enemy pieces (pawns count). Own goals count: if your own piece falls off the end of your line, the point goes to your opponent.
  5. If 20 rounds pass with no ring-out, the ring shrinks by one square on every side (8×8 → 6×6 → 4×4 → 2×2). Everything left outside is out and scores for the opponent; a king caught outside loses. If a shrink catches both kings, or takes both players to 6 or more, the higher score wins; if the scores are tied, the bigger army wins (at least a pawn's worth ahead), otherwise it's a draw.
  6. No en passant. A pawn that pushes onto the last rank promotes; a piece pushed there stays what it is.

Base rules (every experiment)

  • Pieces move like normal chess.
  • No check, no checkmate: capture the enemy king to win.
  • Castling only needs an unmoved king and rook and an empty path.
  • 50 rounds without a capture, pawn move or destruction? The bigger army wins (equal armies: draw). Same after 150 rounds.
