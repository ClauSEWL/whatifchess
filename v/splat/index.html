Experiment 06 · Splat Chess

What if chess pieces left paint everywhere they went?

  1. Every unpainted square a piece crosses or lands on takes its color. Forever.
  2. Rooks and bishops paint whole lines. Knights splash only where they land.
  3. Capture the king, or own more of the board after 20 rounds.

Full rules

The twist

  1. Every unpainted square a piece passes through or lands on takes its color, permanently: paint never covers paint.
  2. Rooks, bishops, queens and kings paint their whole path. Knights paint only where they land. Pawns paint where they arrive, plus the skipped square of a double step. Castling paints the squares the king crosses and lands on, which include the rook's new square.
  3. Capturing the king wins at once. Otherwise, after 20 rounds the side with more painted squares wins. A tie is a draw.

Base rules (every experiment)

  • Pieces move like normal chess.
  • No check, no checkmate: capture the enemy king to win.
  • Castling only needs an unmoved king and rook and an empty path.
  • 50 rounds without a capture, pawn move or destruction? The bigger army wins (equal armies: draw). Same after 150 rounds.

Inspired by Paint Chess by Marc Pagliuca.
